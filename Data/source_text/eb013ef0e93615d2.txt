IMPORTANCE: Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a common disorder characterized by 
repeated upper airway collapse during sleep that is associated with reduced 
quality of life and increased incidence of cardiometabolic disease.
OBJECTIVE: To assess the effects of orforglipron on moderate to severe OSA in 
adults with obesity or overweight, compared with placebo.
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: Two 52-week, phase 3, multicenter, 
double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trials were conducted at 66 
sites in the US, Argentina, Brazil, China, Czech Republic, Germany, Japan, 
Mexico, and Taiwan. Adults with moderate to severe OSA and body mass index (BMI; 
calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared) of 27 or 
higher were enrolled between November 11, 2024, and July 23, 2025. Final 
follow-up occurred on July 30, 2026. Trial 1 included patients not receiving 
positive airway pressure treatment and trial 2 included patients receiving 
positive airway pressure treatment.
INTERVENTION: Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive either once-daily 
orforglipron (maximum tolerated dose: equivalent to 9-, 14.5-, or 17.2-mg tablet 
dose) or placebo.
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary end point was change in apnea-hypopnea 
index (AHI, events/h) from baseline to week 52. Seven key 
multiplicity-controlled secondary end points included change in sleep 
apnea-specific hypoxic burden, the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement 
Information System Short Form Sleep-Related Impairment T-score, and 
high-sensitivity C-reactive protein.
RESULTS: A total of 363 patients were randomized in trial 1 (mean age, 48.4 
years; BMI, 37.4; AHI, 50.7 events/h; 31.4% female) and 349 patients were 
randomized in trial 2 (mean age, 51.1 years; BMI, 38.6; AHI, 57.6 events/h; 
26.9% female). For the treatment regimen estimand, in trial 1, the mean AHI 
change was -17.8 for orforglipron compared with -4.5 for placebo (difference, 
-13.2 [95% CI, -17.7 to -8.7]; P < .001) at 52 weeks. In trial 2, the mean AHI 
change was -17.3 for orforglipron and -5.3 for placebo (difference, -12.0 [95% 
CI, -17.1 to -6.9]; P < .001) at 52 weeks. Of 7 key secondary end points, 6 were 
statistically significant, favoring orforglipron. Gastrointestinal events were 
the most commonly reported adverse events, occurring in 123 (68.0%) participants 
randomized to receive orforglipron vs 68 (37.4%) randomized to receive placebo 
in trial 1 and in 125 (70.6%) participants randomized to receive orforglipron vs 
63 (36.6%) randomized to receive placebo in trial 2.
CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: These results provide evidence for the potential use 
of oral orforglipron to improve sleep-disordered breathing in adults with 
moderate to severe OSA and obesity or overweight.

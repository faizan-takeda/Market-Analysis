PURPOSE: Polycythemia vera (PV) is a myeloproliferative neoplasm characterized 
by erythrocytosis. Rusfertide is a subcutaneous, injectable, hepcidin mimetic 
peptide that suppresses erythrocytosis. The phase III VERIFY trial evaluated the 
efficacy and safety of rusfertide in phlebotomy-dependent patients with PV and 
poorly controlled hematocrit.
METHODS: In VERIFY Part 1a (32 weeks), patients requiring frequent phlebotomy 
were randomly assigned (1:1, double-blind) to receive rusfertide or placebo with 
or without cytoreductive therapy. The primary efficacy end point was clinical 
response (absence of phlebotomy eligibility; weeks 20-32). Key secondary end 
points (baseline to week 32) included mean number of phlebotomies, proportion of 
patients who maintained hematocrit <45%, and mean change from baseline in 
patient-reported outcomes (Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information 
System [PROMIS] Fatigue Short Form 8a [SF-8a] T score and Myelofibrosis Symptom 
Assessment Form [MFSAF] Total Symptom Score-7 items [TSS7]).
RESULTS: Patients (N = 293) were randomly assigned to rusfertide (n = 147) or 
placebo (n = 146). Significantly more patients (n = 113, 77%) achieved a 
clinical response with rusfertide versus placebo (n = 48; 33%; P < .0001). 
Compared with placebo, rusfertide reduced mean ± standard deviation number of 
phlebotomies (0.5 ± 1.2 v 1.8 ± 1.5; P < .0001) and was superior in maintaining 
hematocrit <45% (n = 92 [63%] v n = 21 [14%]; P < .0001). Rusfertide led to a 
significant improvement in the PROMIS Fatigue SF-8a T-score and MFSAF TSS7 
versus placebo (P = .0252 and .0240, respectively). Injection site reactions, 
anemia, and fatigue were the most common adverse events. No deaths were 
reported.
CONCLUSION: Rusfertide significantly improved hematocrit control, reduced the 
need for phlebotomy, and improved patient-reported symptoms versus placebo in 
phlebotomy-dependent patients with PV who were also receiving standard-of-care 
therapy.

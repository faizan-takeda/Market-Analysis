Cutoff 2026-11-21. Period labels: Lilly and AbbVie report calendar years; J&J's FY2025 is the 52 weeks ended 2025-12-28 [CLM-002005]; Takeda's FY2025 is April 2025 to March 2026 [CLM-005000]. No currency conversion is used. Every ratio below is a within-company ratio or a growth rate, so JPY and USD are never compared in absolute terms. Ratios marked *(computed)* are arithmetic on the cited claims, not figures any company reported.

## 2. Why the four cases cannot be ranked on one number

1. **Different cliff sizes.** Humira was 36.6% of AbbVie revenue at peak; Stelara 12.8% of J&J's; Vyvanse 9.9% of Takeda's (FY2023 base) *(computed)* [CLM-003004] [CLM-002022] [CLM-005015]. A smaller cliff is absorbed more easily regardless of management quality, so equal replacement ratios would not imply equal capability *(INTERPRETATION)*.
2. **Different elapsed time.** AbbVie's cliff is observed for three years, J&J's for two, Takeda's for two. A replacement ratio computed at different ages is not a like-for-like measure. The only like-for-like comparison available is year-2 decline: Humira -57.7% (2024 vs 2022 peak), Stelara -44.0% (FY2025 vs FY2023 peak), Vyvanse -52.0% (FY2025 vs FY2023) *(computed)* [CLM-003002] [CLM-002022] [CLM-005015].
3. **Different segment boundaries and acquisitions.** J&J's replacement mixes organic growth with acquired revenue (Intra-Cellular, Abiomed, Shockwave) [CLM-002041] [CLM-002011] [CLM-002027], which the registry does not separate fully; AbbVie's replacement products were pre-existing [CLM-003084]. J&J's "ex-Stelara +12.3%" therefore overstates organic resilience by an amount only partly disclosed (about one point per J&J) [CLM-002075].
4. **Different currencies and fiscal years.** Takeda's JPY series embeds exchange-rate effects (FY2025: -1.7% actual versus -2.7% constant) [CLM-005001], and its FY ends in March, so its FY2025 overlaps the other three companies' 2025 by nine months only [CLM-005000].
5. **Different price mechanisms.** AbbVie's defence was contracting (at least 97% of adalimumab volume retained through March 2024) followed by a CVS Caremark / Cordavis co-branded arrangement; the steepest declines (-41% in 2024, -57% in 2025 for U.S. Humira) followed the April 2024 formulary change, which the registry treats as association, not proven causation [CLM-003007] [CLM-003008] [CLM-003010]. J&J's Stelara faced nine biosimilars including four sold by PBM-affiliated private labels by July 2025, plus a Medicare negotiated price 66% below the 2023 list price from January 2026 [CLM-007016] [CLM-008008]. Takeda has not yet faced its Entyvio event [CLM-005017].

**Comparison status:** valid as a side-by-side description and for year-2 decline; **invalid** as a single "resilience score" or as a ranking of management quality.

## 4. What is contested

- Whether Humira's decline after April 2024 was caused by the CVS Caremark change: the data show timing only [CLM-003010].
- Whether J&J's Stelara absorption is complete or flattered by acquisitions: J&J's own breakdown is partial [CLM-002075] [CLM-002041].
- Takeda's USD 10-20bn peak range for six late-stage programmes is a company scenario. It is not adjusted for probability of success and Takeda says it should not be treated as a forecast or target [CLM-005041] [CLM-014049].

## 5. What is unknown

- The Takeda Vyvanse peak and the U.S. share of Vyvanse's decline (gap).
- Skyrizi's biosimilar timing after 2033 (gap) [CLM-003021].
- Any LOE-adjusted net-price data for Entyvio; whether the Entyvio biosimilar BLAs are blocked by 12-year reference-product exclusivity was not confirmed in the Purple Book [CLM-014051].

# Master comparison: Lilly, J&J, AbbVie and the Takeda public baseline

Cutoff 2026-11-21. This file replaces the v0.1 skeleton. It answers the ten comparison questions, each with evidence, and points to the detailed files. No standardized scores or rankings are used; where a comparison is invalid the file says so. Periods: Lilly and AbbVie calendar years; J&J 52-week years (FY2025 ended 2025-12-28) [CLM-002005]; Takeda FY2025 = April 2025 to March 2026 [CLM-005000]. Takeda amounts stay in JPY; growth rates and shares are compared, not absolute JPY against USD. 2026 is partial [CLM-001032] [CLM-003023] [CLM-002060] [CLM-005011]. Gate 1 status: Lilly pass, J&J and AbbVie conditional, Takeda fail (repairable), so Takeda timeline and business-model gaps carry through.

## 1. Why is Lilly winning where it wins?

Lilly's revenue rose from USD 28.3bn to USD 65.2bn (FY2021 to FY2025), 23.2% a year *(computed)*, because tirzepatide went from first revenue in 2022 to 56.0% of sales [CLM-000133] [CLM-000137] [CLM-001001] [CLM-001003]. Pre-tax margin rose from 21.7% to 39.5% and gross margin from 74.2% to 83.0% while R&D roughly doubled [CLM-001007] [CLM-001005] [CLM-000143] [CLM-000147]. The mechanisms evidenced are: a launched incretin with strong trial results (SURMOUNT-1 weight loss up to 20.9%) [CLM-001056]; capacity spending (capex USD 1.3bn to 7.8bn) after a shortage that ended in 2024 [CLM-000172] [CLM-001066]; a cash-pay channel [CLM-001042]; and a follow-on oral and triple-agonist pipeline [CLM-001030] [CLM-001060]. What the evidence does not show is durability: realised prices fell 15% in Q3 2026 and about USD 900m of that quarter was one-off [CLM-014003]. *(INTERPRETATION)* Lilly is winning in a category where demand exceeded supply; the registry does not show whether the advantage persists once supply and competition normalise.

## 2. What makes J&J's pharma and MedTech combination distinctive, and where is the synergy unevidenced?

J&J is the only one of the four with a MedTech segment (USD 33.8bn) beside Innovative Medicine (USD 60.4bn) [CLM-000253]. MedTech earns a 12.2% segment margin against 36.9%, grew 2.5% in Q3 2026, and J&J announced the Orthopaedics separation [CLM-002004] [CLM-002060] [CLM-002037]. J&J claims cardiovascular leadership across Abiomed, Biosense Webster and Shockwave; no cross-segment revenue synergy is disclosed, so the pharma and MedTech synergy is unevidenced [CLM-002074]. The distinctive, evidenced feature is diversification: the largest brand is 15.2% of revenue and Cardiovascular grew 15.8% [CLM-002073] [CLM-002028]. Offsetting evidence: Impella recalls and the Varipulse pause [CLM-002046] [CLM-002044].

## 3. How did AbbVie absorb the Humira loss of exclusivity?

Global Humira fell from USD 21,237m (2022) to 4,540m (2025), -78.6% *(computed)*, after U.S. exclusivity ended on 2023-01-31 [CLM-003002] [CLM-003000]. Skyrizi plus Rinvoq rose USD 18,179m against a Humira loss of 16,697m; total revenue passed its 2022 peak only in 2025 [CLM-003014] [CLM-003015]. Three mechanisms are evidenced: successors already marketed and extended across Humira's indications [CLM-003084] [CLM-003016]; rebate-for-access contracting that held at least 97% of volume to March 2024, then a Cordavis co-brand [CLM-003007] [CLM-003008]; and patent settlements (Rinvoq entry not before April 2037) [CLM-003021]. Steep U.S. declines came after the April 2024 formulary change, which is an association, not proven causation [CLM-003010]. See [loe_resilience_comparison.md](loe_resilience_comparison.md).

## 4. Who converts R&D into commercial outcomes best?

Metric definitions and results are in [rd_investment_and_productivity.md](rd_investment_and_productivity.md) and [pipeline_and_revenue_dependence.md](pipeline_and_revenue_dependence.md). On R&D intensity, Lilly fell from 24.5% to 20.5%, J&J stayed at 15.6-15.7%, AbbVie rose from 12.6% to 14.9%, Takeda stayed at 14.7-15.0% *(computed)* [CLM-001006] [CLM-000085] [CLM-000015] [CLM-005004]. On the share of FY2025 revenue from molecules approved after 2021-11-21, Lilly is at least 56.0%, J&J at least 3.2%, AbbVie at least 1.8% and Takeda not computable *(computed)* [CLM-001003] [CLM-002024] [CLM-003036] [CLM-003037]. **That comparison is invalid as a productivity ranking**: it rewards new-molecule growth and penalises lifecycle growth (Skyrizi and Rinvoq), acquired assets are mixed with in-house ones (sponsor of record for Elahere is ImmunoGen, for Epkinly Genmab) [CLM-012013] [CLM-012014], approval counts (8, 5, 5, 3) are small and include 2026 approvals funded earlier [CLM-012000] [CLM-012007], and R&D accounting boundaries differ [CLM-000270]. The evidence supports only a narrower statement: Lilly's recent growth comes from a molecule approved inside the window, AbbVie's from molecules approved before it *(INTERPRETATION)*.

## 5. Who has the most defensible technology ecosystem?

The registry cannot support a ranking. Lilly has the largest evidenced build (LillyPod, TuneLab, external model partners) with no outcome shown; J&J has the only regulator records (devices, not discovery); AbbVie has an internal knowledge graph; Takeda is partnership-led with 2026 company-reported operating items [CLM-006004] [CLM-000280] [CLM-014022] [CLM-006032] [CLM-014041]. Of 29 company-level entries, 18 sit at concept, prototype or pilot and none shows a demonstrated outcome [CLM-006054; counts computed from data_ai_maturity_matrix.md — INTERPRETATION]. "Lilly is most defensible" is a *(HYPOTHESIS)* resting on proprietary data (company-estimated at over USD 1bn) [CLM-006055] [CLM-000280]. See [data_ai_maturity_matrix.md](data_ai_maturity_matrix.md).

## 6. Which AI investments are operationally meaningful?

Operational status plus disclosed scale: LillyPod (1,016 GPUs) [CLM-006004], AbbVie's ARCH (124 TB, over 30 million nodes) [CLM-006032], Takeda's 170+ Lessines predictive-maintenance agents and plasma nomograms [CLM-014041] [CLM-005060], and J&J's quality-control tool (qualitative) [CLM-006021]. None has independent evidence of effect on cost, cycle time or revenue. NVIDIA lab, Valkai, Isomorphic and OpenAI are announcements [CLM-000281] [CLM-000285] [CLM-006012] [CLM-006011].

## 7. Who has repeatable discovery and development capability?

Evidence of repetition, not of quality: Lilly eight novel approvals across incretin, immunology, neurology and oncology [CLM-012000] [CLM-012007]; J&J five spanning bispecifics, a TKI, an FcRn antibody and an oral peptide [CLM-012008] [CLM-012012]; AbbVie five, mostly acquired or partnered, with growth from supplements [CLM-012013] [CLM-012033]; Takeda three, with three Phase 3 readouts delivered positive on the 2024 plan [CLM-012018] [CLM-005042]. Repeatability is partly unproven for all: AbbVie's neuroscience record is one approval and one failure, Takeda's replacement set arrived in August 2026, and Lilly's expensed 2023-2025 purchases had no approval by cutoff [CLM-012148] [CLM-005029] [CLM-009000]. Attrition appears in every company: see [strengths_and_vulnerabilities.md](strengths_and_vulnerabilities.md) and [rd_investment_and_productivity.md](rd_investment_and_productivity.md).

## 8. Which partnerships create durable options?

Option structures exist at AbbVie (ADARx, Calico, Gubra, Ichnos), Takeda (Innovent licence, equity and option) and Lilly (Beam opt-in, CVRs) [CLM-009061] [CLM-009068] [CLM-009062] [CLM-009060] [CLM-009078] [CLM-014058] [CLM-009099]. No option from the window has yet produced an approved product, so durability is a *(HYPOTHESIS)* [CLM-009095] [CLM-009097]. Realised value from partnerships so far: J&J Momenta (IMAAVY) and Intra-Cellular (CAPLYTA MDD), Lilly Loxo (Jaypirca), AbbVie ImmunoGen (Elahere), Takeda HUTCHMED (Fruzaqla) and Protagonist (Mimrylo) [CLM-009044] [CLM-009031] [CLM-009015] [CLM-009056] [CLM-012018] [CLM-012020]. Deal-value comparisons are invalid across bases; see [partner_ecosystem_comparison.md](partner_ecosystem_comparison.md).

## 9. What does each do better than the other two, and where is each vulnerable?

See [strengths_and_vulnerabilities.md](strengths_and_vulnerabilities.md). In one line each, on evidence: Lilly combines growth with margin expansion and capacity build [CLM-000137] [CLM-001007] [CLM-000172]; J&J absorbs a large LOE with a diversified base [CLM-000253] [CLM-000254]; AbbVie replaces a dominant franchise with pre-built successors [CLM-003014]; Takeda has a plasma-derived franchise and in-house orexin discovery [CLM-005063] [CLM-005048]. Largest vulnerabilities: Lilly, concentration of 56.0% and price erosion [CLM-001003] [CLM-014003]; J&J, talc and a still-falling Stelara [CLM-002062] [CLM-002060]; AbbVie, successor concentration at 42.3% *(computed)* and a USD 4.5bn neuroscience write-down [CLM-000266] [CLM-003057]; Takeda, Entyvio at 21% with exclusivity loss ahead, flat revenue and the AMITIZA provision [CLM-005009] [CLM-005017] [CLM-005001] [CLM-005014].

## 10. What would change positions by 2035? *(HYPOTHESIS; anchors are evidenced dates)*

- Lilly: tirzepatide patent to 2036; Trulicity patent 2027; Trulicity and Verzenio negotiated prices from 2028; retatrutide, lepodisiran and orforglipron outcome trials read out 2029-2031 [CLM-000250] [CLM-008015] [CLM-001047] [CLM-001050] [CLM-001045].
- J&J: Stelara erosion through 2026-2027, talc resolution needing 95% participation, the Orthopaedics separation 18-24 months after October 2025, DARZALEX concentration (patent dates not in the registry) [CLM-002060] [CLM-002062] [CLM-002030] [CLM-002073].
- AbbVie: Vraylar patent September 2029 (analyst source), Skyrizi and Rinvoq composition patents 2033, Rinvoq entry 2037, obesity and in-vivo CAR-T programmes at Phase 1 [CLM-003029] [CLM-003021] [CLM-003073] [CLM-003072].
- Takeda: Entyvio exclusivity loss in the latter half of the decade and 2032 patents, Trintellix generics after December 2026, launch execution of oveporexton, rusfertide and zasocitinib, Amitiza appeal [CLM-005017] [CLM-005016] [CLM-005035] [CLM-014048].
- All four: Medicare negotiation rounds and government pricing agreements [CLM-008015] [CLM-008037].

# Competitor comparison: Lilly, J&J, AbbVie and the Takeda baseline

Cutoff 2026-11-21. Lilly and AbbVie report calendar years. J&J FY2025 is the 52 weeks to 2025-12-28 [CLM-002005]. Takeda FY2025 is April 2025 to March 2026 [CLM-005000]. 2026 figures are partial periods [CLM-001032] [CLM-005011]. Takeda stays in JPY, so growth rates and shares are compared and absolute JPY is never set against USD. The comparison is descriptive; no score or rank is given. Full detail: [master_comparison.md](../New_mediciine/cross_company_analysis/master_comparison.md).

**Audit corrections applied.** AbbVie and J&J "long-term debt" series include the current portion; non-current is USD 58,941m (AbbVie) and USD 39,438m (J&J) at FY2025 [CLM-000070] [CLM-000132]. OTTAVA has an announced first installation site, not a completed install [CLM-000284]. AbbVie ImmunoGen is USD 9.2bn net of cash, and ImmunoGen plus Cerevel USD 17.5bn net [CLM-003086]. Takeda's cell-therapy impairment is JPY 58.2bn, not 58.0bn [CLM-005025] [CLM-009084]. The restructuring cost series is about JPY 150bn, 170.0bn, then 160.0bn [CLM-005078]. Morphic is USD 3.2bn headline against a 2.55bn audited charge [CLM-009002]. The Lilly lab sold to Arctoris has no supported Strateos link [CLM-006017].

## Answers to the ten comparison questions

**1. Why is Lilly winning where it wins?** Revenue rose from USD 28.3bn to USD 65.2bn (FY2021-FY2025) [CLM-000133] [CLM-000137] because tirzepatide went from launch in 2022 to 56.0% of sales [CLM-001003]. Pre-tax margin rose from 21.7% to 39.5% [CLM-001007]. Capex rose from USD 1.3bn to USD 7.8bn [CLM-000168] [CLM-000172]. The evidenced mechanisms are a launched incretin with strong trial results, capacity built after a shortage, a cash-pay channel and a follow-on pipeline [CLM-001042] [CLM-001030]. Durability is not shown: realised prices fell 15% in Q3 2026 and about USD 900m of that quarter was one-off [CLM-014003]. *(INTERPRETATION)* Lilly is winning in a category where demand exceeded supply; the registry does not show the advantage persisting once supply and competition normalise.

**2. What is distinctive about J&J's pharma and MedTech combination, and where is the synergy unevidenced?** J&J is the only one of the four with a MedTech segment (USD 33.8bn beside Innovative Medicine USD 60.4bn) [CLM-000253]. MedTech grew 2.5% in Q3 2026 and J&J announced the Orthopaedics separation [CLM-002060] [CLM-002037]. J&J claims cardiovascular leadership; no cross-segment synergy is disclosed [CLM-002074]. The evidenced feature is diversification: the largest brand is 15.2% of revenue [CLM-002073]. Offsetting evidence: the Impella recall and the Varipulse pause [CLM-002046] [CLM-002044].

**3. How did AbbVie absorb the Humira loss of exclusivity?** Global Humira fell from USD 21,237m (2022) to 4,540m (2025), -78.6% *(computed)* [CLM-003002]. Skyrizi plus Rinvoq rose by USD 18,179m against a Humira loss of 16,697m, and total revenue passed its 2022 peak only in 2025 [CLM-003014] [CLM-003015]. Three mechanisms are evidenced: successors already marketed and extended across Humira's indications [CLM-003084]; rebate-for-access contracting that held at least 97% of volume to March 2024 [CLM-003007]; and settlements putting Rinvoq entry no earlier than April 2037 [CLM-003021]. Steep U.S. declines (-41% in 2024, -57% in 2025) followed the April 2024 formulary change, an association rather than proven causation [CLM-003010].

**4. Who converts R&D into commercial outcomes best?** No ranking is supported. R&D intensity moved from 24.5% to 20.5% at Lilly, held near 15.6% at J&J, rose from 12.6% to 14.9% at AbbVie and held 14.7-15.0% at Takeda *(computed)* [CLM-001006] [CLM-000085] [CLM-000015] [CLM-005004]. The share of FY2025 revenue from post-2021 molecules (Lilly at least 56.0%, J&J at least 3.2%, AbbVie at least 1.8%) is invalid as a productivity measure: it penalises lifecycle growth, mixes acquired and in-house assets, and Takeda is not computable [CLM-012013] [CLM-012014] [CLM-000270]. *(INTERPRETATION)* The narrow supportable statement is that Lilly's growth comes from a molecule approved inside the window and AbbVie's from molecules approved before it.

**5. Who has the most defensible technology ecosystem?** The registry cannot rank. Lilly has the largest evidenced build (LillyPod, TuneLab) with no outcome shown [CLM-006004] [CLM-000280] [CLM-006007]. J&J holds the only regulator records, and they concern devices [CLM-014022] [CLM-014026]. AbbVie has the ARCH knowledge graph [CLM-006032]. Takeda is partnership-led [CLM-006039] [CLM-006041]. *(HYPOTHESIS)* "Lilly is most defensible" rests on proprietary data the company estimates at over USD 1bn [CLM-000280] [CLM-006055].

**6. Which AI investments are operationally meaningful?** Operational with disclosed scale: LillyPod (1,016 GPUs) [CLM-006004]; ARCH (124 TB, built by fewer than ten full-time staff) [CLM-006032]; Takeda's 170+ Lessines predictive-maintenance agents [CLM-014041]. None has independent evidence of effect on cost, cycle time or revenue [CLM-006054, wording stronger than its sources support per audit]. The NVIDIA lab is an announcement [CLM-000281].

**7. Who has repeatable discovery and development capability?** The evidence shows repetition, not quality. CDER novel approvals in the window: Lilly 8, J&J 5, AbbVie 5, Takeda 3 [CLM-012000] [CLM-012007] [CLM-012008] [CLM-012012] [CLM-012018]. AbbVie's are mostly acquired or partnered, with growth from supplements [CLM-012013] [CLM-012033]. Takeda delivered three positive Phase 3 readouts on its 2024 plan [CLM-005042]. Repeatability is partly unproven everywhere: Takeda's replacement set arrived only in August 2026 [CLM-005029] [CLM-005032].

**8. Which partnerships create durable options?** Option structures exist at AbbVie (ADARx USD 335m upfront) [CLM-009061], Takeda (Innovent USD 1.2bn upfront) [CLM-009078] and Lilly (CVRs) [CLM-009099]. No option from the window has produced an approved product, so durability is a *(HYPOTHESIS)* [CLM-009097]. Realised partnership value so far: J&J Momenta (Imaavy), Lilly Loxo, AbbVie ImmunoGen, and Takeda's Protagonist asset [CLM-009044] [CLM-009031] [CLM-012151].

**9. What does each do better than the other two, and where is each vulnerable?** See the table below.

**10. What would change positions by 2035?** *(HYPOTHESIS; anchors are dated claims.)* Lilly: tirzepatide patent to 2036, Trulicity patent 2027, Medicare prices for Trulicity and Verzenio from 2028 [CLM-000250] [CLM-008015]. J&J: Stelara erosion and talc resolution [CLM-002060] [CLM-002062]. AbbVie: Skyrizi and Rinvoq composition patents 2033, Rinvoq entry no earlier than April 2037 [CLM-003021]. Takeda: Entyvio exclusivity loss and 2032 patents, Trintellix generics after December 2026, launch execution, the Amitiza appeal [CLM-005017] [CLM-005016] [CLM-014048].

## Comparisons we refused to make, and why

| Comparison | Why refused |

| Single LOE "resilience score" or management-quality ranking | Cliffs differ in size (36.6%, 12.8% and 9.9% of revenue), age, acquisitions and price mechanism [CLM-003004] [CLM-002022] [CLM-005015] |
| Novel approvals per R&D dollar | Counts of 3 to 8, 2026 approvals funded earlier, acquired sponsors, different R&D boundaries [CLM-012000] [CLM-012013] [CLM-000270] |
| New-molecule revenue share as productivity | Penalises lifecycle growth; Takeda not computable [CLM-012147] |
| Profit-margin ranking | US GAAP with special items against IFRS with a company core measure [CLM-002016] [CLM-003041] [CLM-005014] |
| Leverage ranking | No EBITDA for the three U.S. companies; the Takeda 2.6x could not be located by the audit [CLM-005005] |

| Patient-programme size | Different units: members, patients, cumulative recipients [CLM-007036] [CLM-007004] [CLM-007037] |
| Robotics "leader" | Product, lab and plant robotics are different things [CLM-006054] |
| Defensible-ecosystem ranking | No outcome evidence for any company [CLM-006054] |
| Takeda against Lilly, J&J or AbbVie on any strategy file | The audit bounds cross-company comparisons used by the bets [CLM-012013] [CLM-009098] [CLM-005007] |

## What the comparison means for Takeda

*(INTERPRETATION)* The one mechanism that transfers cleanly from the peer record is the pre-built successor: AbbVie's replacement worked because the successors were already launched across the incumbent's indications [CLM-003084]. Takeda's counterpart (zasocitinib breadth, BET-05) is the best-evidenced bet in the set. Lessons and their transfer verdicts: [lessons_for_takeda.md](../New_mediciine/cross_company_analysis/lessons_for_takeda.md). The strategy is in [takeda_strategy.md](takeda_strategy.md).

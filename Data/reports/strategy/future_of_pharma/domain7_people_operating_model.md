# Domain 7: People and operating model (A11b, wave 3)

Research cutoff 2026-11-21. Takeda FY2025 = April 2025 to March 2026. Options are labelled EVIDENCE_BACKED or HYPOTHESIS; none is an endorsement. This is the thinnest domain in public evidence.

Framing rule: this file does not treat workforce change as replacement of people by automation. Roles change when tasks are automated; the evidence below separates efficiency programmes, which Takeda describes in its own terms, from AI and automation effects, which are unquantified.

Problem addressed: build the skills, governance and structure to use data, AI and new modalities while changing the cost base. *(INTERPRETATION)*

## What the evidence shows
**Competitors**
- J&J: nearly 900 generative-AI use cases were reviewed for risk, with 10-15% driving about 80% of value, and AI project ownership moved to departments to focus on high-value use cases (CIO interview relayed by a newsletter) [CLM-006018, CLM-006019]. 47,000 employees completed a required generative-AI course, which A10 treats as a training metric, not a deployment [CLM-006022]. Maturity: self-reported pilot to operational.
- AbbVie: ARCH was built by fewer than ten full-time staff plus contractors, with a governance decision to open data to all R&D [CLM-006032, CLM-006034].
- Lilly: a chief AI officer organisation exists, with compute and data investments but no workforce data [CLM-006003, CLM-006007].

**Takeda**
- 80% of the digital workforce was external when the transformation was designed, leading to large-scale insourcing through internal "digital factories" (executive interview via BCG, interested source) [CLM-005057]. Gabriele Ricci has been CDTO since February 2022 [CLM-005056].
- A March 2026 transformation targets over JPY 200bn gross annual savings by FY2028 through streamlining corporate functions, moving teams closer to patients and customers and process simplification; the cost series is JPY 150bn, 170bn then 160bn in successive company figures, and trade press reported about 4,500 role reductions (under 10% of roles) [CLM-005059, CLM-005078]. The May 2024 programme booked JPY 128.1bn and JPY 70.8bn of restructuring expense in FY2024 and FY2025 [CLM-005077].
- Julie Kim, with PDT and US commercial background, became CEO on 24 June 2026 [CLM-005073, CLM-005074]. Synthesis: [CLM-011078, CLM-011079].

**Contrary evidence and caveats**
- The share of the JPY 200bn attributable to technology versus other levers is unknown (IVB-003) [CLM-005078]. J&J's value-concentration figure rests on one interview [CLM-006019]. Digital-factory insourcing has no reported outcome [CLM-005057].

## Where proprietary advantage exists
- Governance, training and operating cadence are slow to copy but not secret; Takeda's advantage would be organisational (decision speed, stop discipline) rather than a technology asset *(INTERPRETATION)* [CLM-005025].
- Partnering is better for scarce specialist skills (AI model building) via the alliances already in place [CLM-005049].

## Success criteria
- Number of AI use cases in production with owner, baseline and measured benefit at 12 months *(HYPOTHESIS)*.
- Proportion of savings attributed to technology versus other levers, disclosed internally (closes IVB-003) *(HYPOTHESIS)*.
- Retention and internal-mobility rate in roles affected by automation *(HYPOTHESIS)*.

## Risks
- Execution load: a CEO transition, a restructuring and several launches in the same period [CLM-005073, CLM-005074, CLM-005078].
- Morale and capability loss if efficiency and AI are conflated in communication *(INTERPRETATION)*.
- Adoption-failure risk is disclosed by competitors (AbbVie 10-K) [CLM-006036].

## Evidence and gaps
Gaps: Takeda headcount by function, AI skill inventory, retention data, J&J and Lilly workforce effects, any independent study of AI productivity in pharma. See [evidence_gaps.md](evidence_gaps.md).

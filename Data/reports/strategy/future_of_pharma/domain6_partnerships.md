Problem addressed: refill a pipeline and add platforms faster than internal research alone, at tolerable capital at risk. *(INTERPRETATION)*

## What the evidence shows
**Competitor patterns (deployed deals with disclosed terms)**
- Lilly splits deals into early asset purchases expensed as IPR&D (DICE USD 1.9bn, Morphic USD 2.5bn, Scorpion USD 1.4bn, others), capability purchases (POINT, Verve, NexPharm USD 925m) and ecosystem platforms (TuneLab, NVIDIA lab) [CLM-009095, CLM-009012, CLM-009026, CLM-009029].
- J&J makes a few large commercial or late-stage purchases (Abiomed USD 17.1bn, Shockwave USD 11.5bn, Intra-Cellular USD 14.5bn) plus smaller platform buys [CLM-009096].
- AbbVie combines two large purchases (ImmunoGen USD 9.2bn net, Cerevel USD 8.3bn net) with a steady stream of USD 130m-2.1bn early-stage assets and options (ADARx USD 335m upfront) [CLM-009097, CLM-009061].

**Outcomes (audited)**
- Write-downs: AbbVie emraclidine USD 4.5bn of USD 8.7bn; J&J Ottava USD 0.9bn and bermekimab about USD 0.8bn; Lilly DICE DC-806 discontinued after a USD 1,916m charge; Takeda JPY 58.2bn, 27.8bn and 21.5bn [CLM-009100, CLM-009054, CLM-009003, CLM-009083, CLM-009084].
- Approvals: J&J Momenta (nipocalimab, FDA approval 2025-04-29; company announcement 04-30), Intra-Cellular (CAPLYTA MDD, about seven months after the 2025-04-02 close) [CLM-009044, CLM-009031]. Value destruction concentrates in neuroscience, gene and cell therapy and robotics [CLM-009100].

**Takeda's pattern**
- One flagship asset purchase (Nimbus, USD 4.0bn upfront plus two USD 1.0bn sales milestones), then licences, options and equity (Innovent: USD 1.2bn upfront including USD 100m equity, up to USD 11.4bn total), and write-offs of earlier purchases; Takeda states no significant acquisitions in the three years to March 2026 [CLM-009076, CLM-009078, CLM-009087, CLM-011075]. Four of six late-stage programs are externally sourced [CLM-005050].
- Ecosystem machinery: the Center for External Innovation is on the BaseLaunch board; Takeda Ventures scope is not public [CLM-009091, CLM-005052]. Terminations show partner risk: Neurocrine ended TAK-041/831; Takeda ended the Denali collaboration in April 2026 [CLM-009090, CLM-009089].

**Contrary evidence**
- J&J closed JLABS sites (Houston, Toronto, Washington DC, New York reported) [CLM-009052]. The AbbVie Ventures founding date conflict (2009 versus about 2015) is unresolved, so ecosystem-arm claims are inconclusive [CLM-009074]. Synthesis: [CLM-011076].

## Where proprietary advantage exists
- Proprietary advantage is clinical execution plus selection skill (deal choice and stop decisions), shown by Takeda's three late-stage approvals or filings on schedule and discipline in stopping soticlestat and cell therapy [CLM-005042, CLM-005024, CLM-005025].
- Partnering is better for new modalities, AI models and automation, where evidence of internal advantage is absent and open or shared models exist [CLM-006056, CLM-006058].

## Success criteria
- Share of Phase 3 and filing assets sourced externally that reach approval at or above the in-house rate, tracked by deal *(HYPOTHESIS)*.
- Contingent payments committed versus data-gated milestones achieved, per AI partnership *(HYPOTHESIS)*.
- Impairments as a percentage of deal value over a three-year window versus AbbVie (54% of Cerevel net price) [CLM-009054] *(HYPOTHESIS)*.

## Risks
- Single-asset concentration (Nimbus) and binary Phase 3 risk [CLM-009076].
- Partner termination (Denali, Neurocrine) [CLM-009089, CLM-009090].
- Antitrust and policy review of large transactions *(gap; not researched in this module)*.
